## 1. Orientation & district thesis

**The thesis: the old road and the bypass.** US 50 and Old State Route 74 were the working roads east out of Cincinnati — truck stops, motor courts, feed stores, hamlet main streets. Then the **SR 32 Appalachian Highway** went through (built out through the '60s–'70s) and took the traffic. By 1995 the old corridor is a string of bypassed towns making do, while the Eastgate interchange booms at the district's western edge and suburbia advances east toward it. **Highway Kin hold the roads progress abandoned.** Every location choice below should read as "still in use, never repainted."

Unlike Price Hill's vertical tiers, this district is **two parallel east–west corridors with north–south stitches**:

| Band | What | Character |
|------|------|-----------|
| North corridor | **US 50** — Milford → Day Heights → Perintown → Owensville | The old road. Hamlet-flat-hamlet rhythm. Kin country. |
| South corridor | **SR 32** — Eastgate → Batavia bypass → east | The four-lane bypass. Fast, patrolled, anonymous. |
| Western boundary | **Little Miami River valley** + I-275 crossing | Scenic river, rail-trail, the loop's high bridge. Gateway band. |
| Northwest ring | **Miami Township** — SR 28 corridor, Day Heights, Mulberry, Miamiville/Branch Hill | The suburbanizing ring around Milford city — the front's second axis (§3f). |
| Eastern edge | **East Fork State Park / Harsha Lake** | Big woods and water. Where things get hidden. |
| Stitches | SR 132, SR 222, SR 28, Stonelick roads | Two-lane north–south rural connectors. |

## 3. Sub-area character sheets

### 3a. Milford — old town on the river
Brick Main Street at the historic river ford, 1995 condition: tired antique shops, a diner, half the storefronts thin. The US 50 bridge into town is the front door. Across the river: Terrace Park money, ten minutes and a universe away (same contrast trick as Delhi vs. Lower Price Hill). **Tone: a town that used to be on the way to somewhere.**

### 3b. The US 50 corridor — Day Heights, Perintown, Owensville
Flat bottomland farm fields punctuated by crossroads hamlets, motor-court ruins, and the Kin's clubhouse. Stonelick Creek country to the north with a real one-lane covered bridge. **Tone: heat-shimmer flats, gravel shoulders, dogs off the chain.**

### 3c. Batavia — county seat
The county's courthouse, jail, sheriff, and bail-bonds row concentrated in a bypassed village — SR 32 runs right past without stopping. Every Kin member has been through this courthouse; some of their fathers too. The dormant Peavine rail line rusts through the middle of town. **Tone: the county's fist, and it's still a poor town.**

### 3d. East Fork / Harsha Lake — the big woods
Dammed reservoir (1978) and its state park: boat ramps, campground loops, gated service roads, remote coves. Where the Kin stash what needs stashing and meet who can't be seen. **Tone: beautiful and empty, which is the point.**

### 3e. Eastgate edge — the advancing front
The 1980 mall and its parking ocean at the I-275 interchange. New strip development creeping east along SR 32. Not Kin ground — it's what's coming for Kin ground. **Tone: everything the Kin's territory isn't; fluorescent, patrolled, new.**

### 3f. Miami Township — the ring around Milford
The township wraps Milford city on the east bank of the river: Day Heights and Mulberry crossroads, the river hamlets of Miamiville and Branch Hill up the valley toward Loveland, and the **SR 28 corridor**, where the first big-box strip anchors are landing mid-decade on what was farm field. New cul-de-sac subdivisions are going in behind them — half-built streets, framed houses, mud lots. This is the suburban front's **second axis**: Eastgate advances along SR 32, and this advances along SR 28, with Kin country in the pinch between. The Kin don't hate the township — half of them went to vocational school here — but every new rooftop is ground they don't get back. **Tone: fresh asphalt over old field lines; the future arriving in phases.**

## 5. Gameplay points of interest

### Chokepoints
| Where | Why it works |
|-------|--------------|
| **Stonelick Covered Bridge** | One lane, wooden, weight-limited. The district's signature squeeze — local knowledge says take it at speed; pursuit has to brake or gamble. |
| **Milford US 50 bridge** | The only river crossing into Old Milford — hold it and the town is closed. |
| **I-275 high bridge** | Long exposed span over the valley — nowhere to turn, everything visible. |
| **Courthouse square, Batavia** | One block of Main St with the county's entire law apparatus watching. Standoffs, bail-out pickups, "ride past slow." |
| **Harsha dam service road** | Gated at both ends; a meeting there is a commitment. |
| **Round Bottom Rd pinch** | River on one side, quarry cliffs on the other, gravel trucks in between. |
| **Rail-trail bollards** | Every road crossing is a bike-width gap — free for the Kin, a wall for cars. |
| **Terrace Park radar gauntlet (G1)** | Not a physical pinch — a legal one. Speed traps between the Kin and the city; run it hot and heat follows you into the district. |

### Sight lines / overwatch
| From | Sees |
|------|------|
| Promont House hill | Milford, the river bridge, the valley |
| Harsha dam crest | The whole lake and eastern woods |
| The Scales' CB mast (climbable platform) | The Perintown flats both directions on US 50 |

### Natural chase routes
1. **Old road run:** Milford Main → Day Heights → Perintown flats → Owensville. Hamlet pinches alternating with wide-open flats — rhythm chase.
2. **Covered-bridge cutback:** bait pursuit off US 50 onto Stonelick Williams Corner Rd, thread the bridge, lose the cruiser.
3. **Quarry run:** Round Bottom Rd at dusk — dust, gravel trucks, surface changes.
4. **Bypass pull:** flat-out on SR 32's four lanes with state patrol closing, then bail at the Old 74 exit into streets the OSHP doesn't know.
5. **Rail-trail escape:** bike-only, river-flat, bollard gaps — the local-knowledge route.
6. **Lake loop:** campground gravel → boat-ramp switchbacks → dam gate finale.
7. **The loop ritual:** I-275 over the high bridge and around — the Kin's patrol run toward the Hamilton half (G3), scored to CB chatter.
8. **Strip-to-dark:** SR 28 from the bright big-box end through Mulberry into rural black — pursuit visibility collapses mid-chase. Bail into Phase II's unfinished streets at your own risk: half of them dead-end in mud.
9. **Valley quiet run:** Loveland-Miamiville Rd through the river hamlets, rail-trail alongside — the low-profile surface route north when the loop is too hot.

### Stakeout spots
The Relay's window booth (US 50 traffic), bail-bonds row (who's getting out), airport fence line (what lands at the cargo hangar), fairgrounds barns (off-season business), dam gate (who drives a gated road), mall lot edge (who's meeting under lights).

## 7. Period accuracy — 1995 checklist

- **SR 32 is fully open and Eastgate is booming** — the suburban front is advancing east along the highway. The Kin can see it coming; the old road already lost.
- **The SR 28 strip is just arriving** — first big-box anchors mid-decade, subdivisions in phases behind them. Build the Miami Township ring (§3f) as *in transition*: construction fencing, fresh curb cuts, field lines still visible behind the new lots. Don't name specific 1995 storefronts — the corridor is real, the tenants are ours.
- **Ford Batavia Transmission is running** (opened 1980; period-correct as a major employer). Treat as neutral economy anchor.
- **No cell phones.** Payphones: Milford Main diner, the Relay, fairgrounds office, Batavia courthouse square, Eastgate mall entrance. **CB radio is the Kin's net** — real trucker/rural culture of the era, and the faction's whole comms identity.
- **The Little Miami Scenic Trail is fresh** — newly-paved rail-trail stretches with bollards, still novel in '95.
- **Canoe season:** summer weekend civilian traffic on the river and US 50.
- **County fair late July** [R] — the district's one big crowd event.
- **Radio flavor:** WEBN from the city fading in and out, country stations local, high-school football on Friday nights (Milford Eagles).
- **Tone guardrails:** eastern Clermont's rural/Appalachian-edge poverty was real — trailer courts, salvage frontage, thin storefronts. Play it straight, same rule as the Price Hill doc: communities under pressure, not caricature. The pleasant geography (river, lake, farm flats) stays pleasant — the *buildings* carry the hard-used fiction, per faction direction.

## 8. Blockout notes for the build

1. **Terrain is gentle** — rolling flats with two carved features: the Little Miami valley (west boundary, the one real elevation drop, crossed by the I-275 high bridge) and the Harsha Lake basin (east). Don't invent hills; this district's drama is horizontal.
2. **Corridor pacing is the level design.** The hamlet-pinch / open-flat rhythm on US 50 creates natural speed zones without props. Compress fields, keep the rhythm.
3. **Two-corridor structure:** build US 50 and SR 32 as genuinely parallel routes between the same endpoints (Eastgate ⇄ east edge) so every east–west journey is a choice: slow-and-known vs. fast-and-watched.
4. **Surface variety carries the rural feel:** asphalt (aged on US 50, fresh on SR 32), gravel (Round Bottom, campground, salvage yard), dirt (fair oval), boards (covered bridge), rail-trail pavement.
5. **Covered bridge needs its collision/weight script** (§6) and a visible posted-limit sign so the pursuit asymmetry reads as fair.
6. **Rail-trail bollards** at every road crossing — bike-width gaps, physically blocking cars, real-world plausible.
7. **The Scales' CB mast** should be visible from most of the US 50 corridor — it's the district's wayfinding landmark the way St. Michael's towers serve Lower Price Hill.
8. **Existing scene note:** same caveat as the PH doc — current TurfNode positions are abstract placeholder coordinates; plan this district's real coordinates before relocating TurfNode_Milford (§6).
